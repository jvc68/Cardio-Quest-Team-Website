Project Description

Hypertension is globally estimated to affect 1.4 billion adults, with only 25% of that population having it under control. This problem disproportionately affects the indigenous communities, facing prevalence rates that are 10-20% percent higher than non-indigenous groups as a result of systemic healthcare inequalities and cultural mismatches in medical intervention. Our client, Dr. Jared Duval, aims to address this issue with CardioCare Quest, an NIH funded mobile application that replaces the isolating and “clinical” feel of current health apps to encourage a culturally grounded form of play. Rather than a simple tracker, CardioCare Quest allows users to track heart health related data by participating in culturally-relevant health games. Our role in this project is to build the Twine-integrated app shell, allowing users to “write” their own cultural narratives directly into the game for others to play. Additionally, we will be designing a number of games based on workshops conducted alongside community members. This app lays the foundation for future interventions that are culturally resonant and community-led alongside reducing health disparities for groups that have historically been underserved by both healthcare and technology.

Envisioned Solution

Our solution consists of a Unity-based app shell that manages the user state, navigation, and clinical telemetry. Within this shell, we are implementing a game architecture where individual health factors such as diet, exercise, and medication are addressed through distinct games.

Implementation Details

  • Authoring Module: By integrating Twine, we are creating an "open" architecture that allows community members to update the game with their own cultural narratives and assets.
  • Telemetry Hook: A custom script within Unity parses Twine variables and logs them as clinical data points.
  • Experience Module: Users navigate an interface that visualizes progress through narrative progression and story milestones.

Design Process

  • Our team will utilize a phase-based development approach. This begins with requirements acquisition via meetings with Dr. Jared Duval as well as team leads for the CardioCare Quest and NetGauge projects.
  • The first phase of our process will be focused on design and testing, where we will iteratively refine Figma designs and further discuss our progress in meetings with the lab.
  • The second phase will shift our focus into development and testing, where we will continuously reference NetGauge’s Twine-Flutter design to develop our own Twine integration. Additionally, we will employ the “Next 5” planning approach to foster weekly prototyping and progress meetings.Subsequently, we will still steadily be discussing and receiving feedback from the lab regarding development.
